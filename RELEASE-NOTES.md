# Dial release notes

Newest first. Downloads: https://github.com/JKS-sys/dial-08-oct-2026-releases/releases/latest · https://ipconfig.co.network/dial

## Dial 1.0.33 — 06 Nov 2026

- **Boost up to 2000M % (2,000,000,000 %, +146 dB) with Pro**, up from 20M %. The popover gets a sixth chevron (teal, with its own sound and drawn scene), the menus offer 200M % and 2000M %, and typed volumes, dial:// links and hotkeys all go that high. Dial’s boost guard still keeps it from clipping, but levels like this can damage speakers and hearing — turn it up slowly.

## Dial 1.0.32 — 06 Nov 2026

- **Export as .md or .txt** next to CSV in every Owner panel table. Crash reports come out as a readable report: one section per crash with its kind, version, system and hits, then where it happened, the whole stack and the symbolise command.
- **Dispatch finds every connected line.** It only looked at Ethernet / Wi-Fi ports named the usual way, so a second landline on PPPoE (ppp0) or an adapter macOS lists differently was missing. It now lists every link that is up with an internet address (VPN tunnels, AirDrop and links without an address are left out).
- **Fixed a crash on the Studio's Camera tab** in 1.0.31 (“Can't find variable: cs”) — the new Center Stage switch's state was kept outside the tab that draws it.
- **The internet never stops with Dispatch.** If dispatch stopped by itself, or Dial quit, crashed or updated while it ran, the Mac stayed pointed at a proxy that was gone and nothing loaded. Dial now turns the SOCKS proxy off the moment dispatch stops, puts it back at the next launch if it was left on, and only ever turns off its own proxy (one you set up yourself is left alone).

## Dial 1.0.31 — 06 Nov 2026

- **The camera no longer zooms in by itself.** macOS Center Stage (on an iPhone used as a webcam, a Studio Display and other cameras that have it) zoomed in and followed you on the preview, the stream and recordings. It is now off for Dial unless you turn it on with the new Center Stage switch on the Studio’s Camera tab.

## Dial 1.0.30 — 06 Nov 2026

- **New log boxes** for the stream log, the recorder log and the Dispatch log: every line marked and coloured by what it says (errors red, warnings orange, done green, info sky), and inside each line numbers, units, times, paths, links, quoted text, keys and each kind of bracket in its own colour (no pink). A drawn icon that writes itself (with a heartbeat while live), new lines slide in, error lines flash, counts of errors / warnings / successes in the header, one-click copy, and a soft sound when an error or success line arrives.
- **Lighter in the background:** decorative pulses, glows and shimmers pause while Dial is not the front window (meters keep moving), and the sidebar fits itself in about 5 layout passes instead of up to 20.
- **AirPlay outputs work with Dial running** (a MacBook Pro as an AirPlay receiver, a HomePod, an Apple TV). macOS can’t send AirPlay through Dial’s private audio path, so the app was silenced and nothing reached the receiver. Apps playing to AirPlay are now left to macOS (Dial’s per-app effects are off for them), and an AirPlay receiver in a multi-output list no longer stops the other outputs.
- **Live streams no longer drop after about an hour.** Nothing kept the Mac awake while live, so an idle Mac went to sleep on its timer and the stream dropped and reconnected. While live or with Dial Camera on, Dial now keeps the Mac from idle sleep (the display can still turn off).
- **Beauty stays on the stream while your face is hidden for a moment.** A hand in front of the face or a fast turn dropped skin smoothing to a quarter, so the plain camera showed through; it now keeps most of it until the face is found again. While live, a frame the GPU misses keeps the last beauty frame for up to 5 s (was 1.5 s).

## Dial 1.0.29 — 06 Nov 2026

- **Live stream keeps the beauty look.** When the Mac’s graphics engine resets (sleep, heavy load, Dial in the background), Dial waited 3 s before rebuilding the beauty pass and sent the plain camera meanwhile — and after three resets beauty stayed off. It now rebuilds within a few frames, keeps trying, and the stream holds the last beauty frame instead of showing the plain camera.
- **No more password prompts while Dial works.** Fan changes and Auto Boost, Low Power Mode, freeing memory, the Time Machine boost and the update hand-over each asked for the administrator password on their own. Now every one of them goes through the Dial helper: the first time one is needed Dial installs the helper (one password, once), and after that nothing asks again. If you cancel, Dial does not ask again until it restarts.

## Dial 1.0.28 — 06 Nov 2026

- **Camera: no more trail of hands when you move.** Anti-flicker and noise reduction mixed half of the previous frame into every new one, so a moving hand left fading copies behind it. Both now leave moving parts of the picture alone and only smooth what is still; anti-flicker evens out brightness only.
- **Menu-bar popover: big boosts are readable.** The slider’s knob sat on top of the number (200M %); the slider now stays in its own space and the number is larger.
- **Dispatch tab scrolls again.** Its status check ran on the app’s main thread and froze the window every 3 s; it now runs in the background, reads the network links at most every 10 s and pauses while Dial is hidden.
- **Updates stop asking for the administrator password.** A Dial.app owned by the system made every update ask “allow administrator access for a script started by Dial”. Dial now gives the app to you once — silently when the Dial helper is installed, otherwise with one last prompt — and every later update installs without asking.

## Dial 1.0.24 — 04 Nov 2026

- **The boost percentage in the menu-bar popover is readable.** A big boost used to be squeezed into the narrow column at half size (2,000,000 % came out about 6 px tall). From 10,000 % it now shows in short form at full size, like 12K % or 2M %, with the exact value on hover.

- **File paths from another Mac.** Profile and key paths written on a different Mac (`/Users/<someone>/…`) now resolve on this one: the same path under this Mac's home folder, else the same file name in Signing, Downloads, Desktop, Documents, the key folders or `~/Library/MobileDevice/Provisioning Profiles`.

- **The App Store Connect key on any Mac.** A release no longer stops when `APPLE_API_KEY_PATH` names a file from another Mac: it looks where Apple's tools look (`~/.appstoreconnect/private_keys`, `~/private_keys`, Downloads…), or writes the key from `APPLE_API_KEY_P8` in the vault to a private file in the build cache.

- **A new Mac sets itself up from Pour.** The release doctor now installs Rust with Pour (`rustup` and the stable toolchain) when the Mac has none, instead of stopping with a `curl | sh` line, and a broken npm is first reinstalled with Pour (a fresh `node@22`) before anything else is tried.

- **Dial Camera keeps its beauty and stops stuttering.** While Dial Camera was on, every frame was compressed to JPEG in the window and decoded again in the camera, which fought the beauty pass for time. The face finder fell behind, and the face effects kept dropping out, so the plain camera showed through. Dial Camera now takes the GPU's own picture (NV12, converted by VideoToolbox, letterboxed) beside a raw stream, with no JPEG on either side. A briefly missed face is held for 2.5 s instead of 1 s, so a busy moment no longer switches the face effects off.

- **Dial Camera in the Mac App Store version.** The App Store build now carries Dial Camera too (`network.co.ipconfig.dial.store.camera`, sandboxed, with its own Mac App Store profile in `APPLE_STORE_CAMERA_PROFILE`). Without that profile the build goes on without it, and says so.

- **Release keys from the dashboard vault.** `update.sh` and `update.sh --appstore` now read Dial's keys from the ipconfig dashboard's encrypted vault (`/api/v1/owner/vault/export?app=dial`), so the Mac's `.env` needs only `APPS_API_KEY`. A key that `.env` sets still wins, the key travels in a header (never on the command line), values never touch the screen, and the temporary copy is deleted at once. `DIAL_VAULT=0` releases from `.env` alone.

- **Custom RTMP connects.** A Custom RTMP card now sends what nearly every RTMP ingest takes (the same as OBS's generic settings): H.264 Main, at most 1080p 30 and 6 Mb/s, AAC 128 kb/s, no B-frames. It used to send the output's full quality (up to 1440p 60, High profile), and some servers refused it. The card's own Size, Frame rate, Video bitrate and Profile still override each one.

- **Dial Camera and the stream keep going when you leave the Studio tab.** The Studio used to close with its tab, which stopped the camera, Dial Camera and the stream until you came back. While the picture is going out (live, recording or Dial Camera on) the Studio now keeps running in the background on any tab.

- **Security updates.** The TLS library Dial uses for every HTTPS connection (rustls 0.23.46, webpki 0.103.16 and the root certificates) and the updater plugin (2.14, with zip 9) are on their fixed versions; the developer tools (wrangler) too. `cargo audit` and `npm audit` report no vulnerabilities.

- **Crash reports without the noise.** "ResizeObserver loop completed with undelivered notifications" is a browser note, not a crash: older Dials still sent it, so the server now drops it and the hourly clean-up removes the ones already stored; old copies on the Mac are deleted too.

- **Release fixes.** The R2 clean-up no longer guesses 2,024 file names when the bucket can't be listed (it took an hour and miscounted); the App Store build checks the finished package for quarantine flags before upload.

## Dial 1.0.23 — 03 Nov 2026

- **Going live again after Stop connects again.** Right after Stop, a service can still hold the last connection for a few seconds and refuse the new one; Dial used to leave that destination out at once, and with one destination the stream ended. A refused destination is now tried again in a fresh connection after 5, 15 and 30 s, and only left out if it still refuses (a wrong key). A test streams to a server that refuses at first and checks the stream reaches it.

- **No microphone no longer spoils the stream.** On a Mac with no microphone (a Mac Studio with nothing plugged in), or when the microphone can't be opened, the stream now goes on with silence and says so, instead of failing.

## Dial 1.0.22 — 03 Nov 2026

- **The App Store build meets App Review's rules.** It no longer asks for the network-server entitlement (the Studio's phone remote and OSC control are off in that build, with a sentence saying so), and it no longer links the temperature library's private IOHID calls — temperatures there come from the SMC only. `update.sh --appstore` now refuses to upload a build that has either. ## Dial 1.0.21 — 02 Nov 2026

- **Stripchat as a destination, and every destination with its own encoder settings.** Stripchat is in the destination list with its external-broadcasting ingest filled in, and Dial sends it exactly what its OBS page asks for: H.264 Main, zerolatency, no B-frames, 2 s keyframes, at most 1080p 30 and 6 Mb/s, AAC 128 kb/s at 48 kHz — on its own encode, scaled down on its own branch, so YouTube or a recording can stay at 1440p 60. Each destination card now has its own profile, keyframe gap (1–5 s), largest picture size, sound bitrate and a Low-latency switch.

- **"External broadcasting software is offline" with the microphone on — fixed.** A microphone that started late or left gaps kept ffmpeg's FLV muxer waiting for sound, so nothing reached the service and it showed the broadcaster offline. The microphone's sound is now made continuous (gaps filled with silence, drift stretched away) and the muxer never holds the picture back more than half a second waiting for it.

- **The stream follows the camera much more closely.** Live encodes are now low-latency by default — zerolatency, no B-frames, no lookahead and a one-second rate buffer (it was two B-frames, a 10-frame lookahead and a two-second buffer); recordings keep the higher-quality encode.

- **Streaming no longer needs Dial's window open.** WebKit's background throttling is off for the Studio window and App Nap is disabled, so a hidden window keeps composing frames at full rate; closing the window while live, recording or with Dial Camera on only hides it — the stream goes on until you stop it.

- **A real cut-out for the background (blur, colour, picture).** Apple's Vision person segmentation now draws the outline — hair, shoulders, hands, a second person — in place of the old head-and-torso guess from the face box; the matte is smoothed between frames so it doesn't flicker, and the background works even when no face is found.

- **The menu-bar popover is far lighter while it is open.** Its rainbow strip and the Enhance icon's twinkle now run on the compositor instead of repainting every frame: popover idle CPU 20 % → 6 % and style recalcs 60/s → 1.5/s (40 apps: 95 % → 76 %), and its first 5 s of work 675 ms → 210 ms. Sounds and scenes now load in the first idle moment after the window paints, out of the way of start-up.

- **The Owner Panel opens only on the owner's Mac.** The second Mac (`VXPQCY1631`) is no longer an owner: it runs Dial exactly as a customer does (Free, Pro by code or subscription), for testing. The baked owner list, the release's default `OWNER_SERIALS` and the Worker all hold the one owner Mac; a test makes sure the test Mac never becomes an owner by accident.

## Dial 1.0.20 — 01 Nov 2026

- **Studio: a much stronger live stream.** The picture used to reach ffmpeg as a JPEG of every frame, decoded again on the way out (half a CPU core at 720p before any encoding), on a 25-frame clock that made 30 frames a second into 36 (recordings ran 20 % long and the sound drifted), with crushed blacks, x264 only, one bitrate for every service and no way to see why a stream looked bad. Now the GPU hands Dial the raw picture (NV12, the way the encoders want it — no JPEG, nothing to decode, the right colour range), Dial's writer sends it to ffmpeg on a steady clock (no doubled or dropped frames), the Mac's hardware encoder (VideoToolbox H.264, or HEVC for YouTube and custom servers) does the encoding with keyframes every 2 s and a proper buffer — x264 stands in by itself if it fails — and the sound goes up with the picture (128 … 320 kb/s). The quality picker now has 1440p 60 and 4K 60 and uses the services' own bitrates (720p 4500, 1080p60 9000, 4K60 40000); each destination gets what its service takes (Twitch never more than 6000), a **Test the upload** button measures your upload and the time to reach the service and says which quality fits (one click to use it), and the bitrate lowers itself on a congested connection and comes back up when it clears. The Live tab shows the encoder, how the frames go, repeated and skipped frames, congestion, restarts and a stream log; Encoder, Codec, Frames and Adaptive bitrate pickers are new. Measured: ffmpeg's CPU 47 % → 38 % of a core before encoding at 720p30, 360 frames for 12 s instead of 432.
- **Studio: Beauty that looks natural.** The skin smoothing keeps pores and real edges (an edge-keeping blur under the detail, never more than three quarters of the texture taken away — no plastic look), even tone settles blotches and redness without lightening the skin (checked on a real dark-skinned face: the skin's brightness and colour stay within 1 %), eye brightening, whiter teeth and lip tint are subtler by default with a deeper lip colour, the face-shape warps end without a ring, and every preset but Punchy is 15–25 % gentler. A new **Strength** slider scales the whole Beauty stack at once without moving the sliders, and **Compare** now works while live (it is drawn on the preview only, never on the stream). Beauty is now measured on a real person: a photo plus real landmarks (`scripts/face-landmarks.mjs`, `scripts/beauty-real.mjs`), with before / after pictures and numbers.
- **Studio: a Screen studio and a Recorder.** The new Screen tab captures a display, a window or an area through Dial's own engine (ScreenCaptureKit — macOS asks once to allow Screen Recording, and the permission stays across updates) with the system's sound (or Dial's process tap when the capture has none). Layouts put the screen and the camera together — the camera in a corner (any corner, any size), side by side, or the screen alone — and the stream, the recording and Dial Camera all follow, with every Beauty effect still on the camera; the system's sound can go on the live stream mixed with the microphone. The Recorder saves the screen (and the camera, by the layout) as MP4 / MOV / MKV with the hardware encoder and AAC, with Pause / Resume (a paused recording's part is complete at once; Stop joins the parts into one file without re-encoding) and a list of the recordings. New sounds and scenes for the capture, the layouts and the Recorder.
- **macOS will ask for Dial's permissions one more time after this update.** Dial is now signed with the owner's Apple Developer ID and checked by Apple (notarised), so it opens without the right-click → Open dance. The signature is what macOS ties permissions to, so after this one update it asks again for System Audio Recording, the microphone, the camera and Finder — allow them once and they stay, exactly as before. The release script lets this move through once (`release/dr-moved-to-devid.txt` records it) and the next release must keep the Developer ID signature; the per-piece listing says "the planned move to the Developer ID" in plain words instead of "changed". The App Store Connect API key is the first way the release notarises (then the keychain profile, then the Apple ID + app password); each build is stapled and must pass Gatekeeper's own check (`spctl`) before it is packaged. Dial Camera rides along as soon as the Developer ID provisioning profile is in `.env` (`APPLE_PROVISION_PROFILE`); without it the Developer ID release goes out without Dial Camera and says how to get the profile. No Apple keys at all → the self-signed release, as before.
- **Dial for the App Store — a separate build for the Mac App Store.** `bash update.sh --appstore` (never part of the normal release) makes a second app, bundle id `network.co.ipconfig.dial.store`, so it installs beside the website one. There Dial Pro is sold only the way Apple allows: two auto-renewable subscriptions through the App Store (`dial.pro.monthly`, `dial.pro.yearly` — the ids live in `appstore.json`), with the price from the store, Restore purchases, Manage subscription, the terms and the privacy policy on the Subscribe view (Settings → Account & Pro). Razorpay, activation codes, the self-updater (the App Store updates it), Pour, the Donate / Sponsor / GitHub links and the Owner Panel's Codes and Subscriptions tabs are not in that build. It runs in the App Sandbox: per-app volume and EQ (the audio taps), the microphone, the camera, Bluetooth and recordings work there; the privileged helper, fan control, the charge limit, App Tamer, the Finder Quick Actions installer, Dial Camera and streaming through ffmpeg cannot (the sandbox forbids them) and say so in one sentence. The build signs with the Apple Distribution identity, packs a `.pkg` with the installer identity and uploads it with Transporter; a doctor stage names what is missing (`APPLE_STORE_SIGN_IDENTITY`, `APPLE_STORE_INSTALLER_IDENTITY`, `APPLE_STORE_PROVISION_PROFILE`, the API key, Transporter). The Worker keeps a row per App Store subscription after checking Apple's signature chain itself (`POST /dial/api/apple`); the app decides Pro from StoreKit on the Mac. Every step the owner must do in App Store Connect, the review notes and the privacy answers are in `docs/APPSTORE.md`.
- **Both of the owner's Macs open the Owner Panel.** The second Mac joined the first: the app bakes both owner hashes (and whatever `OWNER_SERIALS` in `.env` names at build time — a test checks every serial is covered), `scripts/setup-payments.mjs` defaults to both serials when `.env` is empty, and the Worker's owner API accepts either.
- **The main window asks for everything it needs in one go.** At launch the window used to make ten separate round trips to Dial's own code the moment its page arrived (your preferences, launch info, the audio state and permission, battery, displays, profiles, the screen recorder, Focus). Now one `boot` call fetches all of it, and it is sent while the window's own code is still loading, so the answers are waiting when the first screen draws; later reads still go to the live source as before. The Equalizer view is its own file now, parsed the first time it is opened rather than at every launch (the Mixer's chunk is a quarter smaller).
- **Dial's records are real tables now, in stores named for the app.** The Worker's data moves out of the one-blob `dial` database into the dashboard database `dial-08-oct-2026` (Databases tab) with a table per kind — codes, subscriptions, claims, computers (every Mac that redeemed a code, kept in step with its code), crash reports and a small meta table — each with columns the owner can read and sort, and indexes on everything the Worker looks up; the dashboard stamp and the crash-report counters live in the key-value namespace `dial-kv-08-oct-2026` (Key-value tab). Claims and crash reports still expire (7 and 90 days) and are swept by the hourly tidy-up. The release moves the records in from wherever they are — the `dial` database, the older `dial-kv` namespace or, if the earlier moves never ran on this Mac, Workers KV — the newest store winning any clash, copies right before the new Worker goes live and catches up what the old one wrote meanwhile, and once the move is done deletes the old database, the old namespace and the Workers KV namespace after a last check and a read-back. By hand: `node scripts/migrate-db.mjs --check | --dry-run | --remove-old-stores`. A value that cannot be read as a record is kept in the namespace under its old key and named, never lost. The tests run the Worker against a real SQLite (Node 22.5+) or a small SQL emulation on older Node (`DIAL_FAKE_DB=js|sqlite`), under both.
- **Several boosted apps at once stay audible — for good this time.** With a live stream, two loops and many players all at 2000000 % for more than twelve hours, again only one could be heard. Dial's watchdog was not the cause: the same scene run through its rules for fourteen hours never rebuilt a path and never left an app silent. The cause was arithmetic. Dial does not mix the apps — macOS adds every app's sound on the output — and at these boosts every app sits at Dial's ceiling all the time, so nine apps add up to nine times full scale: a hard clip in which the loudest source at each instant owns the sound and the rest disappear into it. Now boosted apps **share the output**: Dial counts how many apps are making sound on the same output and limits each one to its share of the ceiling, so their sum never clips and every app stays audible. The share moves smoothly as apps start and pause (no clicks, no pumping). It is on by default — Settings → Audio engine → *Share loudness between boosted apps*, also in the Dock, tray and window Sound menus — and off, every app may use the whole ceiling as before.
- **Copy the sound log.** Devices → Sound health has a new button, *Copy the sound log*: every app's sound path in plain words — the gain set and the gain applied, how hard the boost guard is working and its ceiling right now, the app's share of the output, when it was last heard, what the watchdog thinks of it and its last verdicts — plus the engine's counters (paths built and torn down, pipelines alive, rebuilds by cause) and the health log. Paste it into a report.
- **A long-session tidy-up.** Over days of apps coming and going, a few per-app tables in the sound engine kept entries for apps that had quit; they are pruned now, and the engine counts every path it builds and tears down so a leak would show in the sound log.
- **Boost up to 20000000 %.** With Pro (Mac and Linux), any app can now be turned up to 20000000 % (200000 times, +106 dB), ten times the 2000000 % of before. The popover gets a fifth chevron: 100 → 1000 → 10000 → 100000 → 2000000 → 20000000 % and back to 100 %, with its own sound; the menus, the Dock, the tray, hotkeys, links, Quick fixes and Dial AI all reach the new top, and the readouts make room for eight digits. The boost guard keeps the sound under full scale and shares it between boosted apps, and nothing in the engines can overflow at this gain — but levels like these can damage speakers and hearing, so Dial says so and asks you to turn it up slowly. Windows stays at 100 % per app.
- **Free boosts to 500 %.** Without Pro, any app can be turned up to 500 % (five times): the slider's lock now sits at 500 %, the Boost menus offer 150, 200 and 400 % to everyone, and only the steps past 500 % ask for Pro. Windows stays at 100 %.
- **The boost is always in view.** Each app's row in the menu-bar popover keeps its percent beside the slider, in bold orange when it is boosted; the right-click menus head with the app's current level ("Safari · 2000000 % ↑"), and the Dock, menu-bar and tray menus title each app the same way, so a boosted app shows its level without opening anything.
- **No more "ResizeObserver loop" crash reports.** That browser note is harmless; the live hook already skipped it, and now the older local hook and Dial itself skip it too, so it never appears in Crash Reports again.
- **The old key-value stores are removed after the move.** Dial's records live in the dashboard's own database since 1.0.19, and the release now cleans up behind it: once the move is marked done and the new Worker has caught up, the release checks one last time that every key the old key-value store `dial-kv` still holds is in the database (a missing one is copied first; one the new Worker deleted since the switch stays deleted), reads a few keys back from the database at random, and then deletes the dashboard's `dial-kv` namespace and, if one is still on Cloudflare, the old Workers KV namespace `dial-kv` (through the API token in `.env` or a signed-in wrangler — when neither is there it says so and leaves that one for the Cloudflare dashboard). It happens once: the marker then says `cleaned` with the date, and `bash update.sh` reports "old key-value stores removed <date>". By hand: `node scripts/migrate-db.mjs --remove-old-stores`, and `--dry-run` lists what would go. Nothing in Dial names a Cloudflare database of its own any more, and the deploy refuses a `kv_namespaces` or `d1_databases` binding in `worker/wrangler.toml`.

### Install

Dial is a Mac app (Apple silicon — M1 and later). One download: `Dial_<version>_aarch64.dmg`.

- **With Pour:** `curl -fsSL https://ipconfig.co.network/pour/install | sh` then `pour install --cask dial`
- **By hand:** open the DMG and drag Dial into Applications. First launch: `xattr -cr /Applications/Dial.app` (Dial is self-signed, not notarized).
- Per-app volume and the equaliser need macOS 14.4 or later.
- Earlier Windows, Linux, ChromeOS, FreeBSD and Intel Mac builds stay on the releases page but are no longer updated.
