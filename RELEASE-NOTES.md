# Dial release notes

Newest first. Downloads: https://github.com/JKS-sys/dial-08-oct-2026-releases/releases/latest · https://ipconfig.co.network/dial

## Dial 1.0.17 — 26 Oct 2026

- **Boost up to 100000 %.** With Pro (Mac and Linux), any app can now be turned up to 100000 % (1000 times, +60 dB), five times louder than before. The popover's chevrons go 100 → 200 → 1000 → 10000 → 100000 % and back to 100 %; the menus, the Dock, the tray, hotkeys, links, Quick fixes and Dial AI all reach the new top. The boost guard still keeps the sound under full scale with no clipping, but very high boosts can hurt ears and speakers, so turn it up slowly.

## Dial 1.0.16 — 26 Oct 2026

- **Studio: Beauty you can see on the Mac.** The skin smoothing reaches about 1.5× further (4.5 px at 720p, 6.7 at 1080p) with a stronger response, and Even tone now gently lifts the skin, so Studio light reads clearly smoother and brighter than Natural. A background shader compile can no longer stall on WKWebView: if the GPU never says it is done, Dial checks the program itself after 400 ms, and a variant that fails is reported instead of leaving the plain picture on. When Beauty is on but can't run (safe mode, no GPU, a failed shader) the preview says so. A new **Compare** button: hold it to see the camera as it comes in on the left and Dial's picture on the right. The preview no longer has black bars on both sides in wide windows.
- **Studio: every destination can be removed.** Each card (Custom RTMP too) has its own Remove button beside ⋯, and ⋯ → Remove; saved lists with repeated, missing or copied ids are cleaned once on load, and new ids never repeat.
- **Fewer permission prompts after updates.** Every release now also lists each signed piece of Dial (the app, the audio engine, anything inside) with its signature, and the release stops if the audio engine was ever signed in a way that changes with each build.
- **Lighter in the background.** Five more background jobs (saving settings, the idle-app check, system notifications, crash reports) share Dial's one timer thread instead of keeping a thread each, and the main window no longer redraws its header 15 times a second while idle; the level meters only redraw when the level really changes.
- **More right-click.** Each app: Quieter by 10 % and Louder by 10 %. Outputs ▸ Balance ▸: Mono, Swap left and right and Limiter. Quick actions: Copy the devices as text and Unmute everything (apps, output and microphone). In the menu bar, the Dock and the window.
- **Six more Finder actions:** Add an Echo, Make It Sound Like an Old Radio, Make the Stereo Wider, Make a Nightcore Version, Mirror the Video and Make the Video Black and White — each with its own sound and picture.
- **Four more window layouts:** Neon, Pills, Stripes and Paper (28 in all), each with a preview, a sound and a scene.
- **More colour in code and logs:** links, file paths, hex numbers, comments and units now each have their own colour; six more drawings.
- **Smaller app:** SHA-256 and signature checks now use the crypto Dial already ships, so three libraries are gone.

## Dial 1.0.15 — 26 Oct 2026

## Dial 1.0.13 — 25 Oct 2026

- **Studio is much lighter.** The preview no longer copies every camera picture through the processor to look for your face — that alone kept a core busy the whole time. Face finding now runs on the graphics card on a tiny picture, and only when framing, beauty, a background, relight or face exposure needs it. Effects you haven't switched on cost nothing at all, each camera frame is drawn once (not up to four times on a fast screen), and Studio opens with less to load. Leaving Studio gives all its memory and graphics memory back.
- **Releases without a Developer ID.** Until Apple's Developer ID certificate arrives, a release is made the self-signed way (without Dial Camera) on its own, with one line saying so — no extra setting needed. Once the keys are in, they're used; if only some are there, the release stops and lists what's missing. Your shared key names work too (APPLE_SIGN_IDENTITY, APPLE_PROVISION_PROFILE, APPLE_NOTARY_PROFILE, or an App Store Connect API key).
- **No more emoji icons.** Every icon in Dial, its menu-bar readouts and its web page is drawn, and follows the light and dark themes.
- **Dial Camera — a real camera for every app.** Studio's finished picture (framing, beauty, looks, overlays) shows up as "Dial Camera" in Zoom, FaceTime, Meet, Teams, OBS and any other app. Install it once from Studio → Live (macOS asks you to allow it), then switch it on and off; while it is off, apps see a calm Dial card instead of a frozen picture. "Send to OBS" is gone — Dial Camera replaces it. Dial Camera comes with the first release signed with Apple's Developer ID and notarised; with that update macOS asks for Dial's permissions one last time, then keeps them.
- **Studio: overlays you can always remove.** Clicking Add or Remove quickly could leave copies ("Toy status" four times) that wouldn't go away. Every overlay now has its own remove button and a ⋯ → Remove, single ones (level meter, chat box, toy status) can't be added twice, and copies saved earlier are cleaned up the first time Studio opens.
- **Studio: beauty without the white glow.** Eye brightening could light up the middle of the picture (and, with no face found, the wrong place) — the picture was even drawn upside down on the graphics card. Beauty now never makes anything brighter than the camera's own picture, only works where a face really is, and fades in and out instead of flashing. Anti-flicker no longer slowly brightens the picture.
- **Studio no longer brings Dial down.** If something in Studio fails, Studio stops on its own with Restart and Safe mode buttons while the rest of Dial carries on; a busy or reset graphics card no longer freezes the window (the preview draws at its size on screen and gets smaller when the Mac can't keep up — recordings and streams stay full size).
- **Studio: much more to play with.** Even skin tone, whiter teeth, bigger eyes, a V-line jaw, a soft key light from the left or right, exposure and white balance measured on your face, noise reduction and vignette; LUT looks (8 built in, or your own .cube files); picture backgrounds (6 built in, or your own photos, also behind a green screen); **scenes** that switch the whole picture with one click, a hotkey, OSC or the phone; a 3-2-1 countdown before going live; a timer and a chat box overlay; snapshots with their own gallery; recording as MP4, MOV or MKV; a bitrate for each destination; and live graphs of bitrate, frame rate and dropped frames. Every control has its colour and icon, and the new actions their own sounds and animations.

- **Boost up to 20000 % (Pro).** Every slider, menu, hotkey, link, profile, the Dock and the volume HUD go to 20000 %; a new look-ahead limiter keeps even ×200 clean and can never turn sound into silence or noise, and a second hearing warning comes past 5000 % ("this can damage hearing and speakers").
- **No more apps going silent under heavy use (macOS).** With many videos looping, pausing and restarting at once (live streams, YouTube loops, Cine), a sound path that went quiet twice used to stay quiet — the app was muted until it stopped playing. Now a path that can't be fixed within 2 seconds lets the app play on its own, unprocessed, and Dial tries again after 20 s, 1 min and 3 min; a rebuilt path is ready before the old one is removed, so there is no gap.
- **AirPods and other Bluetooth headphones as the speaker.** Choosing them in the menu bar or the window connects them if needed, waits for macOS to make them an output, then switches — and says in plain words when macOS refuses. A connected device that isn't playing yet shows a "Use" button.
- **Your Mac never runs flat on the charger because of Dial.** When Dial held the charge limit by switching wall power off, a restart (or a low limit with a wide range) could leave the power off and the battery drained to 0 % with the charger plugged in. Now the charger always comes back: at start, at quit, after a crash, when Dial's control stops for a minute, when a battery reading fails, under a 20 % floor, and through the background helper even when Dial isn't running. Under 10 % on the charger and not charging, Dial gives the battery its charger back and tells you. Battery → Control shows what Dial is doing right now, with a **Stop controlling** button. Calibration now stops at 15 %, and a discharge no longer carries on after a restart.
- **Brightness keys on any keyboard.** F1 / F2 (and F14 / F15 on Apple-layout keyboards) change the brightness of the external display under the pointer from external keyboards too, and from the Touch Bar — and they work without the Accessibility permission (macOS then also sees the key). - **Fewer permission prompts after updates.** macOS remembers what you allowed (folders, network, recording) by the app's signature; every release is now signed with the same certificate and checked against the previous one before it goes out, so updates keep your answers.
- **The Dial web page and shop no longer run out of their daily allowance.** Their data moved to Dial's own accounts service.
- **Safer by default.** The Studio camera feed for OBS answers only on this Mac (never on the Wi-Fi, never to a web page), and the phone remote's link can't be guessed. Exported settings never contain your AI service keys. A Pro code is never dropped because the server had a hiccup — only when it is still unknown a day later.
- **Faster start, lighter running.** The window comes first: the Dock and Finder hooks, the menu-bar icon, the hotkeys and the mini mixer follow right after it is on screen, so the work before the first picture is about a fifth of what it was (clicking the menu-bar icon early still opens the mini mixer). The Visualizer uses about a third of the CPU (glows without blur), the app tamer and the temperature checks share one timer and wake far less often, and the mini mixer, HUD and camera bubble load about a fifth less style.
- **About the same size with much more inside.** The menu-bar icons are now packed when Dial is built, so the app carries no picture decoder (−137 KB) — that pays for a good part of Studio and the rest of this release, and the Mac download stays about 3.8 MB. Reaching 3 MB would mean dropping the built-in updater and its secure downloads, so they stay.

## Dial 1.0.11 — 19 Oct 2026

- **Several apps playing for hours keep working.** On macOS a sound path that went quiet after sleep or a device change was never checked again, Bluetooth headphones skipped clock-drift correction even when an app played to another device, settings were put back only while a Dial window was open, a busy message line could hold up the sound engine and App Nap could slow it down. Dial now watches every app's path all the time (also after sleep and device changes), rebuilds a quiet or stalled one within seconds and fades it back in, corrects drift whenever the clocks differ, keeps the engine awake while it works, takes a browser's new tabs into its path without a gap, keeps your settings applied with every window closed, and when a path can't be fixed lets that app play normally and tries again after 20 s, 1 min and 3 min. On Linux new streams, devices and default changes reach Dial at once (no polling), every stream of an app gets its settings (also one that starts later) and a sound server that starts after Dial is found by itself; on Windows a new session on another output gets the app's settings.
- **Boost up to 1000 % (Pro).** Every slider, menu, hotkey, link and profile goes to 1000 %; the slider gives the first part to 0–100 % and the rest to the boost in even steps of loudness; a limiter just below full scale keeps loud passages clean. Linux: per-app volume above 100 % was far too loud (400 % was +36 dB) — it now matches the number. Windows keeps each app at 100 % (Windows' own limit) and says so.
- **Even out apps (Pro, macOS).** Brings quiet and loud apps to one loudness before your own volume for each, never boosts silence, never clips, and glides back when switched off. In the Mixer's Apps header, Devices, the menu bar and the Dock.
- **Hearing care.** A "never louder than" limit for each output (70 % suggested for headphones), listening breaks (30 / 45 / 60 / 90 min, optional gentle fade), and today's and this week's listening time.
- **Focus sound (Pro).** The app in front plays at full volume and the others at the level you pick; music can stay as it is, call apps are never lowered, and turning it off gives every app its own volume back.
- **Sound health.** Devices → Sound engine lists what Dial fixed and when ("Dial brought Music's sound back"), with Fix Silent Apps and Copy; notifications say it too, only once sound really comes back.
- **Calls and Auto-pause.** A call app left open no longer lowers your music for ever (only one using the microphone counts as a call), and Auto-pause no longer waits on an app that keeps its output open but plays silence.
- **15 more notifications** — an output coming back or a new one, the microphone switched, a sample-rate change, Bluetooth battery low, a hearing tip when boosting past 400 %, the sleep timer, Even out apps, Focus sound, auto-pause, a call lowering apps, recordings started, the last minute of a free screen recording, the disk filling during a recording and more. They have buttons where the system allows (Unmute, Fix, Open), replace each other instead of piling up, and every kind has an example and its own Test button in Settings → Notifications.
- **Much more in the menu bar.** The mini mixer gains a Sound section (health, Even out, Focus, limits, breaks, Fix) and Recent recordings, a ⋯ menu on every app (volume, boost to 1000 %, output, EQ preset, Even out, reset, the app itself) and menus on every device row with its volume and sample rate. The right-click menu and the Dock menu gain each app's volume, boost, output and EQ preset, every output's volume, the input level, Hearing care, Sound health, Focus sound, What's new, Rate Dial and Window — all with symbols and colours.
- **More right-click everywhere.** Mixer rows gain Boost, EQ preset, Even out, Show in Finder, Hide, Activity Monitor and Copy app info; the window, Mixer, title-bar and sidebar menus gain Focus sound, Hearing care and Sound health.
- **24 more Finder actions (54 in all).** Make Louder, Night-Mode Copy, Podcast-Ready, Reduce Background Noise, Make Mono, Split Left and Right, Opus / OGG / Apple Lossless, Waveform Picture, Trim Silence, Fade In and Out, Speed Up / Slow Down (same pitch), Audiogram Video; for videos Remove Sound, Rotate, Make Square, Smaller for Messages; for folders every video to MP4 and every sound podcast-ready; for apps Mute, Set Volume and Never Touch. New files go next to the original as "name (louder).m4a", never over another file; the same actions are in Linux file managers and Windows Explorer.
- **16 window layouts** (Classic, Icon rail, Peek, Top tabs, Bottom dock, Right, Centred, Compact, Roomy, Floating, Glass, Outline, Bold, Minimal, Studio, Console) with previews in Settings → Appearance, and **8 more list layouts** (20 in all).
- **14 sound packs, 35 new sounds and 54 new drawings**, numbers coloured with their units (°C, W, V, rpm, fps, kbps …), key words coloured in hints and illustrated empty lists.
- **Small windows.** The title-bar volume slider no longer shrinks to a sliver after Monitor, Tamer, Battery, Displays or Thermals was opened, lists and buttons no longer change look after those views load, the sleep timer pop-up shows again, the Owner Panel tables fit as cards, and the last rows of the Displays, Battery and Monitor panels are no longer cut off.
- **Faster everywhere.** The start-up show takes 1.9 s instead of 3.1 s and a click goes straight through to the app; nothing slow runs before the window appears (no system reports, log scans or process lists at launch — the last-shutdown cause is read once per boot); each window loads only its own code (the menu-bar mini mixer starts with about 250 KB instead of 650 KB, the volume HUD with about 110 KB instead of 585 KB) and its fonts are ready for the first paint; sounds and animations load the first time they play; a change no longer re-measures the whole window. ## Dial 1.0.10 — 17 Oct 2026

- **The remaining noise while Dial mixes.** Three more causes found and fixed: macOS's default clock-drift correction between an app's audio and the output added or dropped single samples (heard as ticks and a gritty noise floor) — Dial now asks for the highest-quality correction; an output that changes its sample rate while playing (monitor speakers over HDMI/DisplayPort, Bluetooth during a call, apps that switch to a lossless rate) left Dial's audio path reading at the old rate — Dial now rebuilds the path the moment the rate changes; and on monitors and interfaces with many channels, sound now goes to the device's own stereo channels.
- **Displays: the tab buttons are back.** In some window sizes the Displays tabs showed as empty pills — a size rule hid their labels while another hid their icons. Every tab now always shows its icon and its name in its own colour; when there are more tabs than fit in one row, the bar wraps into two rows (with shorter names only in very small windows).
- **Displays open at once and stay smooth.** Dial now keeps one list of your displays and reuses it — it used to ask every display again (reading each monitor over DDC/CI and waiting for the main thread) on every slider step and every few seconds. Brightness, contrast and volume steps go to the monitor in order, newest value first, so a fast drag never ends on an older value. The Displays view no longer runs never-ending animations in the background.
- **Displays crash fixes.** Fixed a crash when Dial refreshed its DDC/CI monitor connections while a brightness change was still talking to the monitor, and a double release of a virtual screen. Each card and tab in Displays now shows "This part hit a problem — Retry" instead of breaking the window, and a display with missing information can't stop the view.
- **More for Displays.** Safety timers: dimming to black, disconnecting a display, a new resolution or refresh rate and a rotation come back by themselves after 15 seconds unless you press Keep (10 / 15 / 30 s or off in Test). A Colour tab with warmth stops (Candle to Cool) and a colour profile picker (macOS). Day and night schedules per display that can glide over 15 minutes to an hour. HiDPI scale presets (Larger text, Default, More space, Most space). Sleep and wake for DDC monitors, mirror every display to one and stop all mirroring, an Info card from the display's EDID (maker, model, serial, year, size, diagonal, pixel density, refresh ranges) and full-screen test patterns (colour bars, gradients, dead pixels, grid, uniformity, sharpness, black). Every action is in the right-click menus, with its own sound and animation.
- **Better crash reports.** When Dial, its audio engine or its helper crashed, macOS's own crash report was never read, so most crashes could not be traced. Dial now reads its macOS crash reports, keeps a note of every internal error and of what it was doing just before, also notes errors in its windows, and turns each crash into a short report with the exact place it happened. As before, nothing leaves your computer until you press **Send** in the Crash Reports dialog.
- **Brightness keys on external displays.** With the pointer on an external display, F1 / F2 (the brightness keys) change that display's brightness, with the on-screen indicator — ⌥⇧ for fine steps, the built-in display still handled by macOS. macOS asks once for Accessibility (Displays → Tools → Brightness keys; it never asks at launch).
- **Much more in the menus and notifications.** New notifications for devices plugged in, removed or switched, an app playing while muted, the microphone used while muted, recordings saved (a click reveals the file), long file jobs, updates found and installed, profile rules, the sleep timer, a nearly full startup disk and the helper stopping — two new kinds in Settings → Notifications. The menu bar mini mixer gains a Quick strip (profiles, EQ presets, charge limit and fans, sleep timer, recording, tools such as Fix silent apps and Test tone). The menu bar and Dock menus gain Profiles, Recent Recordings, Displays, Battery (with Low Power Mode), Auto Boost, App Tamer, Sleep Timer, Notifications, Settings, Go To, Help and Check for Updates; every view's right-click menu starts with its own actions.
- **Finder Quick Actions.** 30 services in all, including Convert Video to MP4 / MOV / WebM, Make a GIF, Extract Audio as MP3, Play This Folder, Normalize or Convert All Sound in a folder, and Slow / Pause This App in the Background.
- **More layouts.** Dense grid, Two columns, Gallery and Detailed rows (12 in all), remembered per list.
- **Check for updates in Settings.** Settings → General → Updates shows the version, the last check, Check now, Install… and "Check by itself" (at start-up and once a day); also in the app, menu bar, Dock and window menus. Opening a Settings section from a menu now stays on that section while the others load.
- **Smaller downloads.** The Mac disk images and the Linux .deb and .rpm packages are now about a quarter smaller (LZMA and xz compression); the app inside is the same.
- **Tools installed with Pour are found.** Started from Finder, Dial now looks in Pour's folders first for ffmpeg and ffprobe (recording, conversions, Finder Quick Actions).

## Dial 1.0.9 — 17 Oct 2026

- **Noise while Dial processed an app.** On outputs that hand audio over as one mono buffer per channel (many USB interfaces and some combined outputs), Dial copied the app's left/right pairs straight into a mono buffer, so the app played as loud noise. Dial now processes every channel layout properly (left and right go to their own channels). The equalizer also no longer slows down on near-silent sound on Intel Macs, which could cause crackle.
- **Notifications in the notification centre.** Alerts now go to the system's notification centre even while Dial's window is in front (Settings → Notifications → "Also while Dial is in front" turns that off).
- **Your choices stay after a relaunch.** Pausing the System Monitor, its Overview / Processes tab, sort, kind filter and search, the Displays tab and the display you picked, the menu bar mixer's Output / Input tab and its CPU / Memory sort, and the AI panel's tab now come back as you left them. Settings saved by the main window are no longer overwritten by the mini mixer window.
- **Display settings come back by themselves.** Dial already saved HiDPI and the software picture, but put them back only once, 3 seconds after launch — a display that wasn't ready yet (at login, asleep, slow to answer) lost them, and macOS quietly resetting the colour table (wake, Night Shift, True Tone) undid dimming, warmth and contrast. Dial now retries, watches the colour table and puts it back, re-applies everything when a display is reconnected, and also restores brightness, contrast and volume on monitors that forget them (DDC). It never brings a display back darker than 5 %. Mirroring onto a Dial virtual screen is now for the session only, so a restart never mirrors to a screen that is gone.
- **More for Displays.** A new **Presets** tab: Night, Reading, Movie, Presentation and Default for one display or all; **Match brightness** on every display; a **Day and night** schedule (times, day and night brightness, night warmth); **Reset display settings** for one display or all. The Picture tab adds a **Gamma** slider, and Info shows the colour space, connection, preset and whether Dial keeps settings for it. Every action is in the display's right-click menu, with its own sound and animation.
- **Correct temperatures on Apple Silicon.** Thermals, the menu bar and alerts used macOS's averaged sensors (one of them is a fixed calibration value), so CPU temperatures looked wrong. Dial now reads every temperature sensor straight from the Mac's SMC, the way Stats does: each CPU efficiency / performance core, each GPU cluster, battery, SSD, memory, Wi-Fi and palm rest, with friendly names for M1–M5. The menu bar and mini mixer show the CPU core average.
- **Much more Auto Boost.** Presets (Silent, Balanced, Performance, Max cooling), a fan curve with draggable points, which sensor drives it (CPU, GPU, hottest, battery), hysteresis and ramp speed, boost while charging, boost while chosen apps run, mains power only, quiet hours, and fans back to automatic when Dial quits. Thermals also gains per-sensor sparklines, today's min / average / max, a throttling indicator and new animations and sounds.
